# Population-level measurement of LLM-generated / LLM-modified text (state of the art as of 2026-11-14)

Scope note: notes compiled 2026-11-14 for a novelty check of an instrument that compares a post-ChatGPT and a pre-ChatGPT language model, identical except for crawl year (2013-2024), via Delta = log p_new - log p_old, calibrated by LLM-polishing pre-ChatGPT abstracts (2024 arXiv abstracts behave as if ~1/3 LLM-polished; marker words fell 7.5 -> 4.9 per 1,000 words in 2025+ abstracts while Delta did not drop).
Tags: [verified] = primary page (arXiv abstract/HTML or publisher) fetched or its abstract quoted by search this session; [secondary] = only press/aggregator coverage seen; [not re-fetched] = from prior knowledge of the paper, URL given, numbers not re-checked this session; [unverified] = claim seen once or from low-quality source.

Method taxonomy used below:
- (A) Distributional MLE: fit a mixture of a human word distribution (pre-ChatGPT corpus) and an AI word distribution (LLM outputs on the same prompts) to a corpus, solve for mixing weight alpha (Liang et al.).
- (B) Excess-vocabulary / marker-word counting: frequency of words above a pre-2023 counterfactual trend (Kobak et al., Siler, Juzek, Geng & Trotta).
- (C) Detector-based counting: apply a per-document classifier (Pangram, GPTZero, Binoculars, custom) and aggregate, sometimes with error-rate correction.
- (D) Stylometric / interrupted time series on non-lexical features (readability, syntax).
- (E) Causal designs for indirect influence (synthetic control on unscripted speech, lab experiments).
- (F) Language-model likelihood from models of different training dates: no population-measurement paper found (see Key Question 4).

## Key Question 1: Liang et al. distributional GPT quantification (peer reviews, scientific papers) and 2025-2026 follow-ups

### Takeaway
The Stanford/Zou-group "distributional GPT quantification" (method A) is the reference population-level estimator: an MLE over word-occurrence distributions (human reference from pre-ChatGPT text, AI reference from LLM-generated text), reporting a corpus-level fraction of LLM-modified sentences. Headline numbers: 6.5-16.9% of peer-review text at ICLR 2024/NeurIPS 2023/CoRL 2023/EMNLP 2023; up to 17.5% of CS arXiv abstracts by Feb 2024; updated to 22.5% of CS abstract sentences by Sept 2024 (Nature Human Behaviour, Aug 2025); and 2025 extension to consumer complaints (~18%), corporate press releases (up to ~24%), job postings (~10% at small firms) and UN press releases (13.7%). It is a word-frequency likelihood, not an LM likelihood, and it needs a specific "AI reference" LLM.

### Cited Findings

**1a. Liang, Izzo, Zhang, et al., "Monitoring AI-Modified Content at Scale: A Case Study on the Impact of ChatGPT on AI Conference Peer Reviews", ICML 2024 (arXiv 2403.07183, Mar 2024)** [not re-fetched]
- Method: maximum-likelihood estimate of mixing fraction alpha of human vs AI word-frequency distributions (adjectives etc.) at corpus level; no per-document classification. — [arXiv 2403.07183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07183)
- Estimate: between 6.5% and 16.9% of peer-review text at ICLR 2024, NeurIPS 2023, CoRL 2023, EMNLP 2023 "could have been substantially modified by LLMs"; higher in reviews submitted close to the deadline, with lower reviewer confidence and fewer scholarly citations. — [arXiv 2403.07183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07183)
- Limitation: the AI reference distribution comes from a particular LLM and prompting pipeline; the estimate is a fraction of text "substantially modified", not of authors/documents.
- Difference from time-locked likelihood instrument: compares a corpus to two fixed word distributions (human vs generated by a chosen LLM), not to two LMs differing only in training year; vulnerable to the choice of AI generator and to vocabulary shifts.

**1b. Liang, Zhang, Wu, et al., "Mapping the Increasing Use of LLMs in Scientific Papers", arXiv 2404.01268 (Apr 2024), COLM 2024** [not re-fetched]
- Method: same distributional framework applied to 950k+ arXiv, bioRxiv, Nature-portfolio papers (Jan 2020-Feb 2024). — [arXiv 2404.01268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01268)
- Estimate: fastest growth in CS (up to 17.5% of sentences in abstracts by Feb 2024); Mathematics and Nature portfolio lowest (up to 6.3%). Higher LLM modification associated with first authors who post preprints more often, crowded research areas, and shorter papers. — [arXiv 2404.01268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01268)

**1c. Liang et al., "Quantifying large language model usage in scientific papers", Nature Human Behaviour, published 4 Aug 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41562-025-02273-8** [verified via search abstract + author PDF listing]
- Data: 1,121,912 preprints and published papers; abstracts and introductions, Jan 2020-Sept 2024; arXiv, bioRxiv, 15 Nature-portfolio journals. — [Nature Index page](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41562-025-02273-8); [author PDF](https://nlp.stanford.edu/~manning/papers/Liang_et_al-2025-Nature_Human_Behaviour.pdf)
- Estimate: CS abstracts 22.5% of sentences show evidence of LLM modification by Sept 2024, vs physics 9.8%, mathematics 7.8%; Mathematics and Nature portfolio smallest increases; usage higher in CS and among authors from countries with fewer native English speakers. — [Nature Index page](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41562-025-02273-8); [Scimex summary](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/which-scientists-are-using-ai-generated-language-in-their-research-papers)
- Method note: "a statistical model of word frequency shifts, not a per-paper detector"; AI reference built by feeding pre-ChatGPT abstract/introduction snippets through an LLM (two-stage summarize-then-expand). — [search summary of NHB paper](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41562-025-02273-8)

**1d. Liang, Zhang, Codreanu, Wang, Cao, Zou, "The Widespread Adoption of Large Language Model-Assisted Writing Across Society", arXiv 2502.09747 (Feb 2025); published in Patterns (Cell Press), 2025** [verified via arXiv listing + EurekAlert; journal details secondary]
- Data: Jan 2022-Sept 2024; CFPB financial consumer complaints, corporate press releases, job postings, UN press releases. — [arXiv 2502.09747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09747v1)
- Estimates: ~18% of financial consumer-complaint text LLM-assisted by late 2024, adoption surging 3-4 months after ChatGPT release; corporate press releases up to ~24%; job postings just under 10% at small firms (higher at younger firms); UN press releases 3.1% (early 2023) -> 13.7% (2024). — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1099641); [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/nearly-a-quarter-of-corporate-press-releases-are-probably-written-by-ai-now)
- Patents: a parallel patent analysis was inconclusive because of the 18-month publication embargo; only a "(very) moderate uptick" in late-2024 published patents. — [arXiv 2502.09747 v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09747v2)
- Limitation (authors): cannot reliably detect heavily human-edited LLM text or text from newer models -> figures are lower bounds. — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1099641)

**1e. Kusumegi, Yang, Ginsparg, de Vaan, Stuart, Yin, "Scientific production in the era of large language models", Science 390:1240-1243, 18 Dec 2025** [verified via Cornell release + search]
- Method: population-level detector trained by contrasting pre-2023 human papers with AI-written text; author-level adoption date = first manuscript above threshold; >2M preprints (arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN), 2018-mid 2024. — [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/12/ai-gives-scientists-boost-cost-too-many-mediocre-papers); [PDF mirror](https://gwern.net/doc/science/2025-kusumegi.pdf)
- Estimate: post-adoption output +36.2% (arXiv), +52.9% (bioRxiv), +59.8% (SSRN); for LLM-assisted papers, writing complexity no longer predicts acceptance. — [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/12/ai-gives-scientists-boost-cost-too-many-mediocre-papers)
- Critique: a 2026 comment argues the event-study timing is "outcome-triggered" and can produce spurious dynamics. — [PMC13486544](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13486544/) [title only seen; not read]

### Inferences
- The Liang-family estimates (17.5% -> 22.5% of CS-abstract sentences, 2024) are in the same range as the instrument's "~1/3 of text LLM-polished" for 2024 arXiv abstracts, but the units differ (fraction of sentences "substantially modified" vs equivalent polish fraction calibrated on full abstracts). A direct cross-calibration on the same arXiv sample would be a natural validation.
- Method A is itself a likelihood estimator, but over a fixed vocabulary of word occurrences with a hand-chosen AI generator; a time-locked LM pair replaces the "AI generator" with whatever was in the post-ChatGPT crawl, which removes the need to choose one LLM but also makes the target less specific (any 2023-2024 drift is absorbed).

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the Patterns volume/issue/DOI for 1d or whether the Liang group published a 2025-2026 update beyond Sept 2024 data; no post-2024 Liang-method numbers found.
- Did not find a published Liang-method robustness test against marker-word avoidance or against newer generator models (GPT-4o/GPT-5-era).

## Key Question 2: Word-frequency / excess-vocabulary studies (writing, speech, multilingual) and whether markers declined in 2025-2026

### Takeaway
Excess-vocabulary (method B) is the most widely used approach and produces the largest estimates: >=13.5% of 2024 PubMed abstracts (Kobak et al., Science Advances 2025), 57% of 2025 articles from four publishers (Siler, PNAS 2026), and 77% of 2025 PMC full texts / 89% in Dec 2025 (Holzwarth, Gonzalez-Marquez, Kobak, arXiv Aug 2026). Its calibration is contested (Topaz & Bahl, PNAS Jul 2026: "not a calibrated measure of LLM prevalence"). On decline: arXiv evidence shows specific markers ("delve", "intricate", "showcasing") peaked Jan-Apr 2024 and dropped (Geng & Trotta, Findings ACL 2025; Hubarava & Ineichen 2026), consistent with avoidance and/or model-version turnover, whereas PubMed/PMC and Korean-journal studies report markers still rising in 2025.

Adv. PDF via NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10713000)
- Estimate: at least 13.5% of 2024 abstracts processed with LLMs; up to ~40% in some subcorpora (countries, journals, fields). — [arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07016)

**2b. Holzwarth, Gonzalez-Marquez, Kobak, "Most biomedical publications show signs of LLM-assisted writing", arXiv 2608.10715 (11 Aug 2026)** [verified]
- Data: ~1.19M open-access PMC papers 2017-2025 (Jan 23, 2026 baseline). **2c. Siler, PNAS (published ~29 May 2026; DOI reported as 10.1073/pnas.2605754123)** [secondary; DOI unverified]

- Reply (Siler): concedes the excess-mass statistic is "distributional displacement", not an article count; read as prevalence it is a lower bound resting on two assumptions; regression interactions are adjusted differential changes, not causal effects. — [PNAS reply](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2621834123) [abstract/snippet only]

### Inferences
- Divergent marker trajectories by venue: arXiv (CS/ML) shows marker decline after early 2024 (Geng & Trotta; Hubarava & Ineichen; the instrument's own 7.5 -> 4.9 per 1,000 words), while PMC biomedicine (Holzwarth) and Korean KCI (Lee) show continued rise in 2025. Plausible explanations: higher awareness of "AI tells" in CS/ML communities, and model-generation turnover (Lee's provider controls show marker turnover). A marker drop therefore cannot by itself distinguish (i) human avoidance, (ii) newer LLMs with different lexical preferences, (iii) reduced use. A likelihood-based instrument that does not drop when markers drop is evidence against (iii), but separating (i) from (ii) would need generator-version controls (e.g., polish baselines generated with 2024 vs 2025-2026 LLMs).
- Excess-vocabulary estimates span 13.5% (2024 PubMed abstracts, conservative) to 89% (Dec 2025 PMC full texts, "any marker" lower bound) because units differ (share of documents with any trace vs fraction of text). The Topaz & Bahl critique (no calibration on known-origin text) is exactly the gap the instrument's polish-calibration addresses.

### Gaps
- No primary (publisher) text of Siler's PNAS paper or the Topaz & Bahl critique was readable (403/captcha); details are from abstracts and secondary coverage.
- No peer-reviewed 2025-2026 word-level time series of "delve"-type markers on arXiv beyond Geng & Trotta (data to ~late 2024) and Hubarava & Ineichen; the 94% decline figure is from a blog.
- Could not verify the publication venue of Yakura et al. (later version) or whether Kobak's group has published a word-level 2025 decline analysis.

## Key Question 3: Estimates of AI content in news, Wikipedia, web pages, social media, peer review, patents, job ads, complaints

### Takeaway
Outside science, estimates are mostly detector-based (method C): ~9% of new US newspaper articles (summer 2025, Pangram); ~4-5% of new English Wikipedia articles (Aug 2024); Medium and Quora ~37-39% vs Reddit ~2.5% (Oct 2024); ~21% of ICLR 2026 reviews fully AI-generated (Pangram, unaudited); web-wide estimates range from a detector-corrected ~25% of crawlable pages (Aug 2026, secondary report) to >=30% from a keyword method. The distributional method (Liang 2025) covers complaints (~18%), press releases (~24%), job ads (~10%), UN releases (~14%). No dedicated patent-corpus estimate exists.

## Key Question 4: Any work measuring diffusion with LM likelihoods, perplexity trends, or models trained on different time periods; claims of robustness to marker avoidance

### Takeaway
I found no published work (to 2026-11-14) that measures population-level diffusion of LLM-style text with likelihoods from language models that differ only in training-data date. Time-sliced model families exist (DatedGPT: twelve 1.3B models with annual cutoffs 2013-2024, Mar 2026; TypewriterLM history LMs, Jun 2026), but they are used for lookahead-bias or knowledge-cutoff probing, not AI-text measurement. The closest neighbors are (i) Liang's word-distribution MLE, (ii) detector/Binoculars-based counting (two contemporaneous models), and (iii) non-lexical stylometric ITS (Hubarava & Ineichen). No paper claims a model-based measure is robust to marker avoidance; several note that avoidance would bias marker estimates downward and that subtler signals persist.

### Cited Findings
- DatedGPT (Yan, Tang, Gao, Jiang, Lu), arXiv 2603.11838 (v1 12 Mar 2026, v2 23 Jul 2026): twelve 1.3B models trained from scratch on ~100B tokens each with strict annual cutoffs 2013-2024, plus DatedInstruct; purpose: lookahead-bias-free financial forecasting (61,000 firm-day headlines; Sharpe 3.20); "perplexity-based probing confirms that each model's knowledge is bounded by its cutoff year". No AI-text diffusion measurement in the abstract. — [arXiv 2603.11838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11838)
- Pretraining LMs on historical text (TypewriterLM, 7B, cutoff 1913), arXiv 2606.02991 (Jun 2026): history LMs become more "surprised" (perplexity) by post-cutoff events while Llama-3.1-8B stays flat; no AI-text measurement. — [arXiv 2606.02991](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02991v1)
- Hubarava & Ineichen (2026) explicitly use interpretable features and no LM likelihoods/embeddings, and advise against relying on a fixed marker list because "delve"-type markers rise then recede. — [alphaXiv 2610.26008](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.26008)
- Geng & Trotta (2025): avoidance prompting reduces but does not eliminate markers; less conspicuous LLM-favored words ("significant") keep rising, implying influence "persists, potentially in more subtle ways" — the closest published statement to "deeper signals survive marker avoidance", but via word counts, not a model. — [arXiv 2502.09606](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09606v2)
- Holzwarth et al. (2026): conscious avoidance of markers would push excess-vocabulary estimates down; "unclear which effect is stronger" vs human style copying. — [arXiv 2608.10715](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10715v1)
- Lee (2026): marker turnover across LLM generations observed in provider control abstracts -> fixed marker lists drift with model versions. — [arXiv 2609.07447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07447)
- Liang et al. (2024-2025): the distributional estimator is a log-likelihood mixture over word occurrences with a human reference from pre-ChatGPT text, not an LM likelihood. — [arXiv 2404.01268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01268); [NHB 2025](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41562-025-02273-8)
- Detector temporal dynamics: arXiv 2601.04833 studies temporal behavior of zero-shot detection metrics on benchmark texts, not corpus prevalence. [snippet only] — [arXiv 2601.04833](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04833)
- Search for "pre-ChatGPT vs post-ChatGPT model log-likelihood", "perplexity trend of abstracts", and "time-period LMs to detect ChatGPT influence" returned no matching population study (two independent searches, 2026-11-14). — (negative result; no URL)
- Other time-sliced LM families from prior knowledge, none used for AI-diffusion measurement as far as known: TimeLMs (Loureiro et al., 2022, quarterly Twitter RoBERTa), Time Machine GPT (Drinkall et al., 2024), ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT "chronologically consistent LLMs" (He et al., 2025), TiC-LM time-continual web benchmark over Common Crawl dumps 2013-2024 (Li et al., 2025). [not re-fetched/unverified this session]

### Inferences
- Novelty position: the core idea (Delta between a post-ChatGPT and a pre-ChatGPT model that are otherwise identical, calibrated by polishing pre-ChatGPT text) appears unoccupied in the population-measurement literature as of Nov 2026. The infrastructure exists (DatedGPT, TiC-LM-style checkpoints), so the gap is in application, not in model availability. A reviewer would likely compare it against (a) Liang's MLE, (b) excess vocabulary, and (c) Binoculars-type two-model ratios.
- Advantage to emphasize: Delta integrates over all tokens (not a curated list), so it is harder to evade by deleting a few markers, consistent with the observed 7.5 -> 4.9 marker drop without a Delta drop. Risks a reviewer will raise: (1) the post-ChatGPT model also absorbs non-AI temporal drift (new topics, terminology, 2023-2024 events), so same-title AI baselines and placebo years (e.g., Delta for 2019-2022 abstracts across pre-ChatGPT model pairs) are needed; (2) Delta depends on how much AI text was in the 2023-2024 crawl, so it tracks "ChatGPT-3.5/4-era style" and may under-detect newer-generation styles; (3) calibration is on polish of a particular LLM, analogous to Liang's generator-choice issue.

### Gaps
- Did not access full texts of DatedGPT or TiC-LM to confirm whether they ever report perplexity on post-2022 AI-rich text; cannot rule out an unindexed workshop paper using time-sliced models for AI-content measurement.
- No study found that directly tests any measure's robustness to deliberate marker avoidance on real corpora.

## Key Question 5: Evidence that humans adopt LLM phrasing indirectly, and methods separating direct use from indirect influence

### Takeaway
Two lines of evidence support indirect adoption: (1) unscripted spoken language (podcasts) shows abrupt post-ChatGPT increases of LLM-favored words, which cannot be explained by pasting model output, using synthetic control (Yakura et al.) or matched pre/post corpora with synonym baselines (Anderson, Galpin, Juzek 2025); (2) a preregistered experiment (N=496) shows a brief chatbot exchange leads people to adopt its words in their own active vocabulary (Yakura et al., later version). Written-text studies generally cannot separate the two and treat human copying as a bias term (Holzwarth et al.; Siler reply).

### Cited Findings
- Yakura et al. (arXiv 2409.01754, later version): synthetic-control analysis over 824,634 podcast episodes (737,083 hours) screened for unscripted speech causally links the rise of "delve", "showcase", "boast", "intricacies", "meticulous" to ChatGPT's release; preregistered experiment N=496: short chatbot interaction leads participants to adopt its words, persisting past a distractor task and in forced lexical choice. — [alphaXiv v3 overview](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2409.01754v3); [arXiv 2409.01754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01754)
- Anderson, Galpin, Juzek (arXiv 2508.00238): unscripted sci/tech podcasts; AI-associated words rise moderately but significantly post-2022 while baseline synonyms do not; authors caution AI is not proven the sole driver. — [arXiv 2508.00238](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00238v1); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91398460/ai-is-quietly-reshaping-the-way-we-talk)
- Juzek (2026, 34 languages): uptake framed as "consistent with" AI preferences spreading in news writing; no decomposition into direct vs indirect. — [arXiv 2605.25358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25358)
- Holzwarth et al. (2026): human copying of LLM style would inflate estimates; argued to be smaller and slower than direct editing. — [arXiv 2608.10715](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10715v1)
- Siler reply (PNAS 2026): excess mass is distributional displacement, not article-level LLM use, i.e., it mixes direct use and diffusion. — [PNAS reply](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2621834123)
- Geng & Trotta (2025): frames the dynamics as "coevolution" (humans react to LLM style by avoiding publicized words while subtler LLM preferences persist). — [arXiv 2502.09606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09606)
- Lexical alignment with LLM partners in a word game (ACL 2025 short) is related but tests convergence in interaction, not population diffusion. [title/snippet only] — [ACL 2025 short 47](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/2025.acl-short.47.pdf)
- Biomedical "stylistic diffusion": "Are Scientists Starting to Sound like ChatGPT? Stylistic Diffusion in Biomedical Abstracts in the LLM Era", MAKE 8(8):223 (2026), compares 2018-2022 vs 2023-2025 abstracts on LLM-associated style markers. [snippet only] — [DOI 10.3390/make8080223](https://doi.org/10.3390/make8080223)

### Inferences
- The instrument as described ("how much ChatGPT-style text has diffused into human writing") measures the sum of direct LLM polishing and indirect stylistic diffusion; its polish calibration expresses the total as a direct-use equivalent. To separate them, borrowing the spoken-language design (a channel where pasting is implausible, e.g., transcribed unscripted talks, or pre-2022-trained authors' informal text) or author-level panel designs (same authors pre/post, as in Kusumegi et al.) would be the most defensible options found in the literature.

### Gaps
- No written-text study found that quantitatively decomposes direct vs indirect contributions at corpus level; the PNAS exchange and Holzwarth et al. acknowledge but do not estimate the indirect component.
- Venue/date of the later Yakura et al. version (with N=496 experiment) not confirmed.
