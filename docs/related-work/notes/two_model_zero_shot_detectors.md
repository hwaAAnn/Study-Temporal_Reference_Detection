# Two-model / reference-model zero-shot machine-text detectors vs. a time-locked (pre-ChatGPT) reference model (as of 2026-11-14)

Idea under test: Delta(x) = mean_t [log p_new(x_t) - log p_old(x_t)], where p_new and p_old come from an identical-recipe model family (e.g., DatedGPT, 1.3B, one model per crawl year 2013-2024, FineWeb-Edu). p_new is trained on a post-ChatGPT crawl and p_old on a pre-ChatGPT crawl. The family s_lambda = log p_new - lambda * log p_old (lambda in [0,1]) runs from plain likelihood (lambda = 0) to Delta (lambda = 1). The control is the same formula with two pre-ChatGPT years.

Verification legend: "[verified]" means I fetched the primary page or abstract this session. "[search-snippet]" means the claim comes only from a search-engine excerpt of the primary source. "[not re-fetched; prior knowledge]" means a well-known paper whose details I did not re-check this session, so treat its exact numbers and settings as unverified.

## Q1. Which zero-shot detectors use two models or a reference model, how are the models chosen, and does any choose them by training-data date?

### Takeaway
Every two-model or reference-model detector I found chooses its pair along one of three axes. The first is base vs. instruction-tuned/aligned in the same family (Binoculars, LOG-AID, IRM, LAPD, dual-network preference divergence, ReMoDetect). The second is sampling vs. scoring model, or perturbation model vs. scorer (DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT). The third is a smaller or weaker model of the same family or data (MIA "Reference", Carlini et al.). None selects or controls the pair by the training data's crawl date, and none frames the ChatGPT release as the reason the subtraction works. The closest algebraic match is LAPD/IRM, which compute exactly log p_A(x) - log p_B(x), but with A = aligned and B = base of the same checkpoint lineage.

### Cited Findings
**Two-model detectors on the base-vs-aligned axis (closest in algebraic form):**
- **LAPD / "Alignment Imprint: Zero-Shot AI-Generated Text Detection via Provable Preference Discrepancy"**: Junxi Wu et al., arXiv 2604.16923 (v1 18 Apr 2026, v2 2 Nov 2026), "NeurIPS 2026" per the arXiv comments field. Raw score Delta(x) = log P_phi(x) - log P_theta(x), with phi = aligned and theta = base. It is then information-weighted, S(x) = sum_t I_phi(x_t) * Delta(x_t) with I_phi = -log P_phi, and standardized as (S - mu~)/sigma~, where mu~ and sigma~ come from conditional-independence sampling from the base model. The paper decomposes log P_phi* - log P_theta = (1/beta) R(x) + V(x) - C, i.e. an SFT "instructional bias" term plus a preference-reward term. Main pair: Llama2-7B / Llama2-7B-Instruct. Ablations use Falcon-7B, GPT-J-6B and Llama3.1-8B pairs, with average AUROC 89.01 / 92.98 / 96.71, and Llama2-7B is best. Its key "Assumption 1 (Indistinguishability of base model)" treats the pre-trained base model as modelling the broad human-language distribution, so in the aggregate it does not separate human from AI text. The empirical check is approximate: the per-token base-model gap between AI and human text is 0.0853-0.6446, versus a 0.6248 average for the aligned model. The paper says nothing about training-data dates or pre-ChatGPT models. [verified] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16923), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16923), [code](https://github.com/creator-xi/LAPD)
- **IRM, "Zero-Shot Detection of LLM-Generated Text via Implicit Reward Model"**: Runheng Liu, Heyan Huang, Xingchen Xiao, Zhijing Wu (BIT), arXiv 2604.21223 (posted 23 Apr 2026), "NeurIPS 2025" per comments. Score r(y) = sum_i log[pi_instruct(y_i|y_<i) / pi_base(y_i|y_<i)], a DPO-style implicit reward with no length normalization. Pairs: Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct/Llama-3.2-1B, which is best at a 91.77% average versus Log-Likelihood 77.46%, Log-Rank 77.42% and Binoculars 87.67%, and Gemma-2-2B-it/Gemma-2-2B. It describes Binoculars as the only other method that uses two LLMs at detection time. It says nothing on training-data cutoff or temporal issues. Its only nearby remark is that "larger or newer versions of a model do not always lead to better detection performance." [verified] — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21223), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21223v1)
- **"Zero-shot detection of LLM-generated text via dual-network preference divergence"**: Chen, Liu, Tang, *Expert Systems with Applications* (2026). It exploits the distribution shift that SFT and RLHF introduce between two networks, and is evaluated on English and Chinese. I saw only the abstract; the author list comes from LAPD's citation [6]. [search-snippet] — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417426001260); cited in [LAPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16923)
- **LOG-AID: Logit-Based Statistical Features for AI Text Detection** (PAN 2025 shared task, CEUR-WS Vol-4038). Token-level statistics come from a base model (Falcon-7B) and its instruct variant (Falcon-7B-Instruct), and the authors report beating the Binoculars baseline in PAN25. It is a feature-based system rather than a single closed-form score. [search-snippet] — [CEUR paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4038/paper_325.pdf)
- **ReMoDetect** (Lee, Tack, Shin, NeurIPS 2024) uses the reward or alignment gap. LAPD describes it as "training-dependent", since it trains a reward model, so it is not zero-shot in the same sense. [verified only via LAPD's related work] — [LAPD HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16923)
- **ImBD (Imitate Before Detect)** (Chen et al., AAAI 2025) fine-tunes the scoring model to imitate machine style. LAPD lists it as a supervised relative. [via LAPD] — [LAPD HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16923)

**Binoculars (two models, cross-perplexity):**
- **"Spotting LLMs With Binoculars: Zero-Shot Detection of Machine-Generated Text"**: Hans et al., ICML 2024, arXiv 2401.12070. Score = log PPL_M1(s) / log X-PPL_{M1,M2}(s): a ratio of perplexity to the cross-perplexity between an "observer" and a "performer". The default pair is Falcon-7B (observer) and Falcon-7B-instruct (performer), i.e. the same family and tokenizer, base vs. instruct. [formula and pair: not re-fetched; prior knowledge] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12070). Independently, MINT classes Binoculars as an "external reference" method that approximates the human distribution with cross-model entropy, and uses Pythia-deduped pairs for MIA and chat/base pairs for detection. [verified] — [MINT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2). The ratio is a division, not a subtraction, and the pair is chosen by alignment status, not data date.

**Curvature / sampling-based detectors (two models in different roles):**
- **DetectGPT**: Mitchell et al., ICML 2023, arXiv 2301.11305. Score = log p_theta(x) - E_{x~ ~ q(.|x)}[log p_theta(x~)], with perturbations from a mask-filling model (T5). The second model is a perturbation generator, not a reference likelihood. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11305), [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/mitchell23a/mitchell23a.pdf) [URL verified; formula from prior knowledge]
- **Fast-DetectGPT**: Bao et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.05130. Conditional probability curvature: (log p(x|x) - mu~) / sigma~, with the expectation taken under a "sampling" model and log-probs from a "scoring" model. The black-box setup pairs GPT-J (sampling) with GPT-Neo-2.7B (scoring). Both models were trained on the Pile, before ChatGPT, and were chosen for availability and strength, not for date. [not re-fetched; prior knowledge] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05130). MINT confirms that it puts DetectGPT and Fast-DetectGPT in the "text sampling" approximation of P_Q. [verified] — [MINT](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2)
- **DNA-GPT**: Yang et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2305.17359. It truncates the text, regenerates continuations with the suspected source model, and compares n-gram overlap (black-box) or likelihood (white-box). It uses a single model, the suspected generator. [not re-fetched; prior knowledge] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17359)

**Single-model statistics (lambda = 0 end of s_lambda, or token-statistic variants):**
- Log-likelihood, Rank, Log-Rank and Entropy baselines (GLTR lineage; Gehrmann et al., ACL 2019 demo). MINT uses them as Loss/Rank/LogRank/Entropy baselines. [verified that MINT includes them] — [MINT](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2); [GLTR arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04043) [not re-fetched]

[search-snippet] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05540). MINT says it only loosely fits the likelihood-ratio framework. — [MINT](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2)
- **Lastde / Lastde++** (Xu et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.06072): time-series ("multiscale diversity entropy") features of one model's token-probability sequence. - **DivScore** (EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2506.06705): zero-shot detection in specialized domains with a domain-distilled scoring model. Its "reference" adaptation is by domain, not by date. [search-snippet; method details not fetched] — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.971.pdf)

**Reference-calibrated scores in membership inference (MIA):**
- **Reference attack** (log p_target - log p_ref, with p_ref a smaller same-family model) and **Zlib** (log-likelihood / zlib entropy), from Carlini et al., "Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models", USENIX Security 2021. In MINT the Reference attack uses Pythia-70M as reference for every target. [MINT usage verified; Carlini details from prior knowledge] — [MINT](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2), [Carlini et al. arXiv 2012.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07805)
- **Min-K% Prob** (Shi et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.16789) and **Min-K%++** (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2404.02936) are single-model token-outlier calibrations; MINT notes Min-K% "is not a ratio". **ReCaLL** (Xie et al., EMNLP 2024) is the ratio of conditional log-likelihoods with a non-member prefix ("text sampling" class). **DC-PDD** (Zhang et al., EMNLP 2024) calibrates against corpus unigram frequencies ("external reference" class). [classification verified in MINT; bibliographic details from prior knowledge] — [MINT](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2)

**Multi-model feature detectors and "smaller model" scorers:**
- **Ghostbuster** (Verma et al., NAACL 2024, arXiv 2305.15047) passes text through unigram, trigram, GPT-3 ada and davinci, then searches over arithmetic combinations of their token probabilities, including differences and ratios, feeding a supervised classifier. Its models are chosen by strength or capacity, not date. [not re-fetched; prior knowledge] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15047)
- **"Smaller Language Models are Better Black-box Machine-Generated Text Detectors"** (Mireshghallah et al., EACL 2024, arXiv 2305.09859) does cross-model scoring and reports that smaller and partially trained checkpoints make better universal detectors. The checkpoints differ in training steps on the same data, not in data date. [not re-fetched; prior knowledge] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09859)
- A 2026 benchmark that uses GPT-2 and GPT-Neo as perplexity reference models attributes part of the detection signal to overlap between the reference models' training corpora and modern generators' corpora, and finds scale barely matters (GPT-2 Small ≈ GPT-2 XL AUROC on HC3). [search-snippet; not fetched] — [arXiv 2603.17522](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17522)

### Inferences
- In the base-vs-aligned family, the reference (the base model) is meant to stand in for "human" text. LAPD states this explicitly as Assumption 1. A pre-ChatGPT base model satisfies that assumption more literally, because its training data contains essentially no LLM output. The time-locked idea can therefore be framed as replacing the alignment axis with a data-vintage axis, or as cleaning the reference. Both "A" models differ: aligned for LAPD/IRM, versus pretrained on post-ChatGPT web for Delta.
- Most 2023-era detectors (GPT-2, GPT-Neo, GPT-J, OPT, Pythia, Llama-2 scorers) happened to use models pretrained before ChatGPT. This was a matter of availability, not design, so training-data date is an uncontrolled variable across the literature. Llama 2's pretraining cutoff is reportedly September 2022 (Llama 2 model card, not re-fetched this session). LAPD's best pair is Llama2-7B, and the Llama3.1-8B pair (post-ChatGPT pretraining) scores lower. That is suggestive, but confounded by the different instruct models, and LAPD does not analyse it.
- None of these methods uses a mixture weight lambda between the two log-likelihoods. Binoculars uses a ratio, LAPD/IRM/Reference use lambda = 1, and the single-model baselines use lambda = 0. The form log p - lambda * log p_ref is standard elsewhere: contrastive decoding (Li et al., ACL 2023, arXiv 2210.15097) and PMI-style calibration, not re-fetched this session. So the interpolation by itself is unlikely to count as a contribution. It is a useful analysis device.

## Q2. Has any paper used likelihood differences between models trained on pre- vs post-ChatGPT data (time-split models, older vs. newer checkpoints, different knowledge cutoffs) to detect AI text?

### Takeaway
I found none. Targeted searches for pre- and post-ChatGPT model contrasts, time-split or point-in-time model families used for detection, older vs. newer checkpoints, and knowledge-cutoff reference models turned up no detector of this kind, either per-document or corpus-level. Time-locked model families exist and are public (DatedGPT, ChronoGPT, point-in-time FineWeb models, TiMoE), but their papers use them for lookahead-bias-free finance and forecasting, not AI-text detection. The pre/post-ChatGPT boundary does appear in AI-text research, but as a data-level reference corpus for corpus-level prevalence estimation, never as a model-level reference likelihood.

### Inferences
- Under an idealized model, p_old ≈ P_H (human, pre-ChatGPT) and p_new ≈ (1 - alpha) P_H + alpha P_AI, where alpha is the AI share of the post-ChatGPT crawl. Then log p_new - log p_old = log(1 - alpha + alpha * P_AI(x)/P_H(x)), a strictly increasing function of the Neyman-Pearson likelihood ratio P_AI/P_H. This gives a principled reason why Delta (lambda = 1) could be optimal, and why lambda < 1 mixes back in a P_H-likelihood term that does not discriminate. This is the per-document, model-based counterpart of Liang et al.'s corpus-level mixture (1 - alpha) P_H + alpha P_AI. The idea in effect turns Liang's corpus decomposition into a per-text detector, using pretrained density models in place of word-frequency tables. This derivation is my inference and is not taken from any cited paper.
- The main confounds are: (a) non-AI temporal drift between crawls (new topics, entities, vocabulary), which the two-pre-ChatGPT control targets; (b) crawl date ≠ creation date (TiMoE, DatedGPT), which dilutes alpha because later crawls re-include old human pages; (c) FineWeb-Edu's educational-quality classifier may differentially keep or drop LLM-style pages. (c) is untested; I found no source.

## Q3. "Machine Text Detectors are Membership Inference Attacks" (ICML 2026): what does it claim, and does it subsume a reference-model likelihood difference?

### Takeaway
MINT (Koike et al.) proves that the asymptotically optimal statistic for both MIA and machine-text detection is the likelihood ratio Λ(x) = P_M(x)/P_Q(x), with M the target or generator model and Q the human-text distribution. It recasts existing methods as different approximations of the unknown P_Q, via an external reference (Reference, Zlib, DC-PDD, Binoculars) or via text sampling (Neighborhood, DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT, ReCaLL). A time-locked reference model fits MINT's "external reference" category in form: log p_old is a proxy for log P_Q. So the general statistic "log p_A - log p_ref" is not new. MINT does not, however, choose references by date. Its numerator is the generator/target model, not a model trained on post-ChatGPT web, and it never mentions temporal splits. The temporal-reference idea is therefore a new instantiation within MINT's framework, not something MINT already covers.

### Cited Findings
- Title, authors and venue: "Machine Text Detectors are Membership Inference Attacks", Ryuto Koike (Institute of Science Tokyo / UPenn), Liam Dugan, Masahiro Kaneko, Chris Callison-Burch, Naoaki Okazaki. arXiv 2510.19492 (v1 Oct 2025, v2 Feb 2026), listed at ICML 2026. [verified] — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2), [ICML 2026 page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/78893)
- Optimal metric: Λ(x) = P_M(x) / P_Q(x) is proved asymptotically optimal for both tasks, with P_Q the oracle human-written distribution. The advantage bound is sqrt(D_KL(P_Q || P_M)/8). No separate reference model appears in the optimal metric itself. [verified] — [MINT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2)
- Unification: "external reference" approximations of P_Q are Reference (another model), Zlib (compression), DC-PDD (unigram corpus frequencies) and Binoculars (cross-model entropy). "Text sampling" approximations are Neighborhood, DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT and ReCaLL (non-member prefixes). DetectLLM fits only loosely, Min-K% is not a ratio, and Lastde/Lastde++ are not likelihood-ratio approximations. [verified] — [MINT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2)
- Reference choices: the Reference attack uses a smaller same-family model (Pythia-70M for all MIA targets). Binoculars uses Pythia-deduped pairs for MIA and chat/base pairs for detection. Nothing is chosen by training-data date or temporal split. [verified] — [MINT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2)
- The paper does not discuss temporal shift. MIMIR members and non-members come from the Pile train/test splits with 13-gram filtering. [verified] — [MINT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2)
- Empirics: cross-task performance rank correlation is ρ ≈ 0.7 (v2; v1 said ρ > 0.6). Binoculars reaches state-of-the-art on MIA benchmarks. Experiments cover 7 MIA methods and 5 detectors across 13 domains and 10 generators. The MINT benchmark is at github.com/ryuryukke/mint. [verified] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19492), [v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v1)
- Inconsistency: the abstract says "15 methods", but the main text names 7 MIA + 5 detection methods (12) plus 4 baselines (Loss, Rank, LogRank, Entropy). The paper does not reconcile the count. [verified] — [MINT HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2)

### Inferences
- In MINT terms, Delta = log p_new - log p_old has p_old ≈ P_Q, which is the external-reference route. The numerator differs in kind, though. MINT's P_M is the specific generator, a single-model detection target. p_new is a model of the post-ChatGPT web, which implicitly mixes many generators' outputs into a human base. Delta is therefore a generator-agnostic "AI-contamination likelihood ratio", not MINT's Λ for one M. A reviewer could still say "this is the Reference attack with a date-chosen reference". The paper should answer that with (i) the mixture derivation, (ii) the pre/pre control, and (iii) a comparison against a same-recipe smaller or earlier-step reference.
- The closest MIA analogue is a reference model trained without the "member" data (the IN/OUT reference models of LiRA, Carlini et al., IEEE S&P 2022, not re-fetched this session). Here the "member" is "LLM-generated text as a class": p_new has seen it and p_old has not. That reading of the idea as distribution-level MIA, where the post-ChatGPT model has "seen" AI text, fits MINT's thesis and could make a positive framing, since it uses MINT rather than contradicting it.

### Gaps
- I did not read the last ~6k characters of the MINT HTML (appendix tail), so a passing remark about reference-model choice may have been missed.
- I did not verify whether the ICML 2026 camera-ready differs from arXiv v2.

## Q4. Is there work on "temporal" or "vintage" reference models for detection, or on detectors whose performance changes with the scoring model's training-data date?

### Takeaway
No detection paper I found studies the scoring or reference model's training-data date as a variable. The nearest temporal literatures are: (a) MIA benchmarks, where temporal shift between members and non-members is a confound that blind date-based classifiers exploit; (b) task contamination, where zero-shot performance depends on whether a dataset predates the model's training data; and (c) detector generalization to newer generators. Papers with "temporal" in the title (e.g., "Temporal Tomography") mean within-document position, not model vintage.

### Cited Findings
- **Duan et al., "Do Membership Inference Attacks Work on Large Language Models?"** (arXiv 2402.07841). Apparent MIA success "can be attributed to a distribution shift, e.g., members and non-members are seemingly drawn from identical domain but with different temporal ranges." [search-snippet] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07841v2)
- **Das, Zhang, Tramèr, "Blind Baselines Beat Membership Inference Attacks for Foundation Models"** (arXiv 2406.16201). Evaluation sets built on hard cutoffs of evolving web sources introduce a temporal shift. Thresholding dates extracted from the text, or a blind bag-of-words classifier, matches or beats published MIAs (90.5% AUROC on BookMIA). [search-snippet] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16201v2)
- **Meeus et al., "SoK: Membership Inference Attacks on LLMs are Rushing Nowhere (and How to Fix It)"** (arXiv 2406.17975) finds serious language distribution shift between collected members and non-members. Maini et al. (2024) flagged the temporal shift in WikiMIA. [search-snippet] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17975v2)
- **CheckMIABench** (ACL 2026 short paper 30) builds cleaner member/non-member splits for two open LLM families and validates them with blind baselines. [search-snippet] — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-short.30.pdf)
- **"Task Contamination: Language Models May Not Be Few-Shot Anymore"** (Li & Flanigan, AAAI 2024, arXiv 2312.16337). LLMs do better on datasets released before their training-data creation date, which is a training-date effect on zero-shot evaluation, not on detection. A contamination survey (arXiv 2404.00699) notes that post-cutoff evaluation does not always drop, e.g. in code. [search-snippet] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16337), [survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.00699)
- Generator recency: detectors trained on older generators transfer poorly to newer ones. [search-snippet] — [arXiv 2406.15583](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15583v1). "Hitting a Moving Target: Test-Time Adaptation for AI Text Detection under Continual Distribution Shift" (arXiv 2606.25152) frames this as continual shift. [title/snippet only] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.25152)
- False friend: "AI-Generated Text is Non-Stationary: Detection via Temporal Tomography" (arXiv 2508.01754) is about statistics changing along a document (28% of AI vs. 15% of human texts are non-stationary), not about model training dates. [search-snippet] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01754)
- Survey for further candidates: Wu et al., "A Survey on LLM-Generated Text Detection: Necessity, Methods, and Future Directions" (*Computational Linguistics*; arXiv 2310.14724). [URL from search] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14724), [CL version](https://fah.um.edu.mo/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/coli_a_00549.pdf)

### Inferences
- The MIA temporal-shift literature makes one specific threat concrete: a pre/post-ChatGPT model pair will also encode non-AI temporal language drift, the very signal blind baselines exploit. The pre-ChatGPT/pre-ChatGPT control, e.g. a 2020 vs. 2021 pair with a similar year gap, is the right ablation. Ideally it should be gap-matched, and it should also include a post/post pair (2023 vs. 2024) to show that the effect tracks AI prevalence rather than calendar distance. Evaluating on human text written after the old model's cutoff but before ChatGPT, or on human text from the same post-ChatGPT period, would further separate "newness" from "AI-ness".
- Because nobody has varied the scorer's data date, existing results from Pile-era scorers (GPT-Neo, GPT-J, Pythia) and post-2023 scorers (Llama-3, Qwen) are not comparable on this axis. A same-recipe time series like DatedGPT is what makes the question answerable, and that is part of the novelty.

### Gaps
- I found no study measuring how zero-shot detector AUROC varies with the scoring model's pretraining cutoff while holding architecture and data pipeline fixed. Such a study would be the direct empirical precedent; none was found.
- No source quantifies the share of LLM-generated pages in post-2022 FineWeb or FineWeb-Edu crawls (alpha above). Without it, the expected effect size of Delta cannot be predicted from the literature.

## Q5. Novelty verdict: is "subtracting a time-locked pre-ChatGPT reference model's log-likelihood" new as of 2026-11-14?

### Takeaway
The verdict is "novel in the choice and justification of the reference (date-chosen, identical-recipe pair; the ChatGPT boundary as the causal source of signal), not novel in algebraic form." The statistic log p_A - log p_B is already used as a zero-shot detector: LAPD/Alignment Imprint (NeurIPS 2026) and IRM (NeurIPS 2025) on the aligned-minus-base axis, the MIA Reference attack on the big-minus-small axis, and Binoculars as a two-model ratio. MINT (ICML 2026) unifies all of them as approximations of P_M/P_Q. I found no paper that chooses the two models by training-data date, uses a time-locked family to detect AI text, or argues that the pre/post-ChatGPT boundary is what makes reference subtraction informative.

### Cited Findings
- Same functional form, different axis: LAPD's raw Delta(x) = log P_aligned(x) - log P_base(x) [verified] — [arXiv 2604.16923](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16923). IRM's r(y) = sum_i log π_instruct/π_base [verified] — [arXiv 2604.21223](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21223v1).
- Theoretical umbrella: MINT's Λ = P_M/P_Q, with a reference model as one way to approximate P_Q; references are chosen by size or family, not date. [verified] — [arXiv 2510.19492](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19492v2)
- Time-locked families exist but are used only for lookahead-bias-free forecasting or finance: DatedGPT [verified] — [arXiv 2603.11838](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11838v2); ChronoGPT [search-snippet] — [arXiv 2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21206); point-in-time FineWeb models [verified abstract] — [arXiv 2607.11889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11889).
- The pre/post-ChatGPT boundary has been used only as a data reference for corpus-level prevalence, not as a model reference. [search-snippet] — [Liang et al. 2403.07183](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07183v3)

### Inferences
- What reviewers will likely call prior art, and how to differentiate:
  1. LAPD/IRM (same subtraction, base model as the human proxy). The time-locked p_old makes their Assumption 1 hold by construction. p_new differs from an aligned model: it is a pretrained model of an AI-contaminated web, not an RLHF/SFT policy. A head-to-head comparison against LAPD/IRM and Binoculars at matched scale is advisable. Scale matching will be hard, since 1.3B DatedGPT is weaker than 7B pairs; consider reporting both raw AUROC and AUROC per unit compute.
  2. The MIA Reference attack and MINT. Position Delta as a generator-agnostic, distribution-level likelihood ratio, (1 - alpha) + alpha * P_AI/P_H, that is monotone in the NP-optimal statistic (my derivation). The lambda-sweep then shows empirically where the optimum lies.
  3. MIA temporal-shift critiques (Duan et al.; Das et al.). Pre-empt them with gap-matched pre/pre and post/post controls, and with human text written after the old model's cutoff.
- The lambda-mixture s_lambda is a reasonable analysis tool, but on its own it is likely not novel: log p - lambda * log p_ref matches contrastive decoding and PMI-style calibration (prior knowledge, not re-fetched).
- Risk: a paper I did not find, such as a late-2026 preprint or workshop paper, could use "older vs newer checkpoints" of a model family (e.g., Pythia/OLMo snapshots with different data, or Llama-2 vs Llama-3 base) as a detection contrast. A final check in Semantic Scholar or Google Scholar is recommended before submission. Suggested phrasing: "temporal reference model", "knowledge cutoff" + "machine-generated text detection", "pre-ChatGPT model" + "Binoculars", "point-in-time language model" + "AI-generated".

### Gaps
- The verdict rests on web-search coverage (about 20 searches and fetches) and on reading the three closest primary papers (MINT, LAPD, IRM) and DatedGPT in full or in part. It is not an exhaustive scholarly-database sweep.
- I found no empirical evidence, for or against, on whether the identical-recipe pre/post-ChatGPT pair actually beats a same-recipe pre/pre pair. That is the experiment the idea itself proposes.
